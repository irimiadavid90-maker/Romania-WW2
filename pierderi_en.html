The Dismantling of Greater Romania

For Romania, the year 1940 marked the critical point of a painful process of international isolation. Left without the support of traditional allies like France and Britain, and surrounded by hostile neighbors, the country faced an impossible choice: accept massive territorial losses or risk total disappearance as a state. Regrettably, in that summer, Greater Romania, the dream fulfilled in 1918, ceased to exist.

Bessarabia and Bukovina

Immediately after the war with Finland concluded, Moscow turned its attention toward our borders. On June 26, 1940, through an ultimatum note transmitted at midnight, the Soviet Union demanded that Romania cede Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina within just 24 hours.

Although Bucharest attempted to negotiate to buy time, the pressure was overwhelming. The Crown Council had to decide quickly: armed resistance or surrender. To save what remained of the state and protect the army, the majority chose the painful path of accepting the imposed conditions. Thus, Romania lost over 50,000 km² and nearly 4 million people. In addition to the requested territories, the Soviets also illegally occupied the Hertza Region, which had not even been mentioned in their treaties.

The Tearing Apart of Transylvania

The next blow came from the West. Adolf Hitler, more interested in Romanian oil than historical justice, wanted to prevent a war between Romania and Hungary. On August 30, 1940, at the Belvedere Palace in Vienna, the fate of Northern Transylvania was decided.

The Romanian delegation was presented with a fait accompli: either accept the new border or Romania would be wiped off the map. It was a moment of extreme tension, following which we lost over 43,000 km², a territory where more than half of the population consisted of Romanians.

Southern Dobruja (The Cadrilater)

The last piece of this tragic domino fell on September 7, when, through the Treaty of Craiova, Southern Dobruja (The Cadrilater) was ceded to Bulgaria. All these massive losses, accepted without a fight, left deep scars in the hearts of Romanians and led to the collapse of the regime. King Carol II, seen as the primary culprit, was forced to abdicate on September 6, leaving the throne to his son, Michael, and full power in the hands of General Ion Antonescu.

Northern Transylvania

Ceded to Horthyist Hungary through the Second Vienna Award (August 30, 1940). Romania lost 43,492 km² and over 2.6 million inhabitants.

Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina

Annexed by the USSR following the Soviet ultimatums of June 26-28, 1940, as a consequence of the secret Ribbentrop-Molotov Pact.

Southern Dobruja (The Cadrilater)

Ceded to Bulgaria through the Treaty of Craiova (September 7, 1940), including the counties of Durostor and Caliacra.

Territorial Balance Sheet

  • West: Northern Transylvania
  • South: Southern Dobruja (Cadrilater)
