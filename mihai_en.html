King Michael I

If the year 1940 represented the collapse of borders, the date of August 23, 1944, marked the moment when a young king of just 22 years old took the destiny of a nation upon his shoulders. In a Europe still dominated by Nazi terror and with the Red Army already at the gates of Moldavia, King Michael I orchestrated the "palace coup" that saved Romania from total destruction.

The Courage of an Impossible Decision

Contrary to the propaganda that tried to portray him as a minor character, King Michael was the engine of events. Seeing that Marshal Antonescu refused to sign the armistice without Hitler's consent — a fact that would have turned Romania into a devastated battlefield — the King acted decisively.

At 17:00, inside the Royal Palace, after Antonescu categorically declared that he would not leave the Axis, the King uttered the phrase that changed the course of history:

"If that is so, then we have nothing left to do!" — King Michael I, August 23, 1944

The Marshal was immediately arrested, and Romania turned its weapons against Germany, shortening the war by at least 6 months and allowing the recovery of Northern Transylvania.

The Most Falsified Event in Our History

For 45 years, the communist regime completely rewrote the facts to legitimize its own existence, turning August 23 into "the day of liberation by the glorious Soviet army and the Communist Party". The mechanisms of falsification were brutal:

  • Erasing the King: In history textbooks, King Michael's role was completely eliminated or presented as that of a "puppet". The myth of the "antifascist armed insurrection" led by the communists was invented, even though the party was almost non-existent at that date.
  • Inversion of characters: The figures who risked everything (the King and the leaders of democratic parties like Iuliu Maniu) were labeled as traitors, while marginal communist leaders were painted as great strategists.
  • Confiscation of the holiday: August 23 became a parade of Ceaușescu's megalomania, hiding the fact that, in reality, it had been an act of national survival initiated by the Monarchy.

Today, recovering the truth about August 23 is not just an historical exercise, but an act of justice toward the courage of a sovereign who, on a summer afternoon, chose to place the future of his people above his own safety.
